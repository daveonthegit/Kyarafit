## Design Context

### Users

Kyarafit is primarily for serious cosplayers who manage multiple active builds, recurring convention schedules, and large personal costume inventories. They often use the product while sourcing pieces, organizing progress, packing for events, or checking details quickly on a phone during time-sensitive prep. The core job is to replace scattered notes, photos, and spreadsheets with a system that keeps complex cosplay work organized, visible, and trustworthy.

Secondary users may include fashion-oriented costume creators and content creators, but the primary lens for design decisions should be committed cosplayers who value structure, speed, and polish over novelty.

### Brand Personality

The brand should feel:

- Studio-practical
- Fashion-informed
- Assured

The emotional goals are confidence, calm, and creative focus. Kyarafit should feel like a serious tool for craft, not a fan toy and not a generic productivity dashboard. It should communicate competence, taste, and readiness.

### Aesthetic Direction

The visual direction should combine production-minded usability with fashion editorial restraint, while still allowing strong image-led moments that create visual pop.

References to preserve:

- Linear and Trello for clarity of task flow, organization, and fast scanning
- Apple for composure, hierarchy, restraint, and high-trust interaction design
- Clean fashion websites for image-led layouts, elegant typography, quiet confidence, and deliberate visual impact

Anti-direction:

- Anything that reads as AI-generated, templated, gimmicky, or trend-chasing
- "AI slop" patterns such as decorative gradients, empty chrome, over-carded layouts, and generic startup UI styling
- Loud fandom visuals, cute/chibi language, gamer UI tropes, or cluttered dashboards

Theme strategy:

- Both light and dark mode should be treated as first-class experiences now
- Neither theme should feel like an afterthought or a color inversion of the other
- Both modes should preserve the same editorial hierarchy and practical clarity

Image direction:

- Images should support both function and atmosphere
- Product imagery can be archival, useful, and reference-driven in workflow views
- Marketing, hero, and showcase moments should be allowed to create visual pop through scale, composition, and contrast
- Decorative image use is welcome when it reinforces taste, energy, and identity without making the interface harder to scan or use

Accessibility baseline:

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA
- Support reduced motion
- Preserve strong keyboard focus visibility
- Maintain reliable contrast in both light and dark themes
- Avoid color-only status communication where it affects comprehension

### Design Principles

- Build for serious use first. Every screen should help users make progress on real cosplay work with minimal friction.
- Balance editorial beauty with operational clarity. The interface should feel stylish, but never at the cost of scanability, hierarchy, or speed.
- Use imagery as both utility and expression. Photos, references, and visual assets should feel intentional, with enough visual impact to keep the product aspirational as well as practical.
- Prefer calm structure over busy novelty. Restraint, spacing, typography, and order should create the product's personality more than effects or ornament.
- Make trust visible. Users should feel their plans, inventory, and progress are stable, understandable, and recoverable across devices and contexts.
